> **Level 1 Goal:** Understand what Linux is, its 4-layer anatomy, why different distributions exist, and learn to read your terminal prompt.

### What You'll Cover
- Linux history and the GNU/Linux distinction
- The 4-layer architecture (hardware → kernel → shell → applications)
- Distribution families (Debian, Red Hat, Arch)
- Reading your terminal prompt like a pro

### Key Concepts to Remember

**Linux is a kernel, not an operating system.** The kernel manages hardware and provides services to programs. GNU provides the tools and utilities. Together they form a complete operating system. This is why Richard Stallman insists on calling it "GNU/Linux."

**The 4-layer model is your diagnostic framework.** When something breaks, you will check layers from bottom to top: Is the hardware responding? Is the kernel loaded? Is the shell configured? Is the application running? This eliminates entire categories of problems in seconds.

**Distributions are not competitors — they are choices.** Debian favors stability. Red Hat targets enterprise support. Arch gives you total control. None is "better." Each is a different answer to the question: who makes the decisions about what gets installed and when?

> 💡 If you remember nothing else from Part 1, remember this: Linux is files. Everything — hardware, processes, network connections, users — is represented as a file somewhere under `/`. This single idea explains why the command line is so powerful.
