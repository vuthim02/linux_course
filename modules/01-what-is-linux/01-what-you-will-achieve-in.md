## 🎯 What You Will Achieve in Part 1

| Level | Focus | What You'll Learn |
|-------|-------|------------------|
| **⭐ Level 1: Basic** | Linux Foundations | What Linux is, the 4-layer anatomy, distributions, why they exist |
| **⭐ Level 2: Intermediary** | Navigating the System | The filesystem tree, users & permissions, first terminal commands |
| **⭐ Level 3: Advanced** | How Linux Really Works | The path of a command, /proc virtual filesystem, kernel interaction |

### Before You Start
- No prior Linux experience required
- You will need access to a Linux terminal (VM, WSL, or cloud instance)
- Each level builds on the previous — try the practices before moving on

### Why This Part Matters

Every skill you build from here on rests on what you learn in Part 1. Understanding the 4-layer architecture — hardware, kernel, shell, applications — means you will never be lost when something breaks. You will know which layer to look at. That is the difference between guessing and diagnosing.

Knowing your distribution family (Debian, Red Hat, Arch) tells you which package manager to use, which config files to edit, and where to find documentation. It is the map for everything that follows.

> 💡 Spend extra time on Part 1 if you are brand new to Linux. A strong foundation here saves hours of confusion later.
