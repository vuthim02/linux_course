## 📚 The 3 Books Every SysAdmin Should Read (Alongside This Course)

| Book | Why |
|------|-----|
| **The Linux Command Line** by William Shotts | Free online — best terminal reference |
| **Linux Administration Handbook** by Nemeth et al. | The classic — used by real sysadmins |
| **The Phoenix Project** by Gene Kim | Not technical — teaches why sysadmins matter |

### Why These Three?

**The Linux Command Line** by William Shotts is available free at linuxcommand.org. It covers shell scripting, text manipulation, and terminal mastery in a way that is thorough but never dry. If you read one technical book, make it this one. It will reinforce everything you practice in this course.

**The Linux Administration Handbook** (3rd edition) by Evi Nemeth, Garth Snyder, and Trey Price is the bible of Linux sysadmin. It covers networking, storage, security, and troubleshooting with the depth that comes from authors who ran real production systems. Keep it on your desk as a reference, not a cover-to-cover read.

**The Phoenix Project** by Gene Kim is a novel about IT operations. It will change how you think about sysadmin work. You will learn about bottlenecks, work-in-progress limits, and why automation matters — all through a story. Non-technical people love it too, so hand it to your manager.

### Other Worthwhile Resources

- **UNIX and Linux System Administration Handbook** blog and errata updates
- **The Art of UNIX Programming** by Eric S. Raymond (free online)
- **Arch Wiki** — the best documentation for any Linux distribution, not just Arch

> 💡 Books complement this course. This course gives you hands-on practice. Books give you depth and context. Use both.
